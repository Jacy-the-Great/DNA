## Dating Comms Rules (Martini Override)

Show more evidence with fewer words. More words = lower status. Default to statements, never questions. Lead with what I'm doing or what's happening, not a query. Treat any question in a draft as a red flag to remove or rework, and flag it if one slips in.

The thing that actually backfires is not intent, it's intent plus effort. Visible effort over text reads as need. Clean, direct intent from a guy who's fine either way reads as confidence. When in doubt, less effort and more frame, not more.

Asking a question does not just lower status, it kills momentum by handing her the decision and forcing her to commit or drive. A keen girl going cold after a question (even when she was chasing) is this, not lost interest. Check her other signals first: fast story views, "another time?", staying in orbit all mean interest is alive. The move is a clean plan-stating line, not a re-ask or a chase.

**Date invites, two honest formats only:**

1. Real plan, when one genuinely exists (event, group thing, something I'd be doing anyway): "I'll be at [place] around [time], come through." Only when true. Never fabricate a plan to manufacture a reason to invite; a fake frame reads as need wearing a confident costume.
2. Direct one-on-one intent, when there's no real plan: state the intention and pick the slot. "Taking you for that drink Thursday." Not "are you free," not "how about," not "does that work." It's true because I'm naming what I intend to do, and decisive because I picked the day. Wanting to take a woman for a drink is not needy. Disguising it as something else is.

One logistics question is fine once she's already agreed (locking the bar, etc). After any ask: one clean line, then genuinely drop it. No follow-up, no double-text, no chasing a slow reply. If she comes, good. If not, I spent ten seconds.

Don't optimise the wording. Spending real energy getting one message perfect is itself the tell. Build the actual life and real plans, and the invite becomes a fact I mention rather than a thing I engineer.

Text default only; in-person is normal.
